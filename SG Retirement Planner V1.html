1. Real return rates

All projections run in real (inflation-adjusted) terms using the Fisher equation. Nominal rates are converted to real monthly equivalents.

Real monthly return = (1 + nominal_rate)^(1/12) / (1 + inflation)^(1/12) − 1 Applied to: Investable assets → investment return slider Cash & emergency → cash/savings return slider Property → property appreciation slider Income growth → (1 + growth%)^(1/12) − 1 per month

2. FIRE number

Based on the 4% rule. The FIRE Number is the portfolio size from which you can withdraw your annual retirement expenses indefinitely at the chosen safe withdrawal rate.

FIRE Number = Annual retirement expenses ÷ Safe withdrawal rate Example (defaults): S$45,000 ÷ 0.04 = S$1,125,000

3. FIRE age — monthly precision via interpolation

FIRE age is detected on the combined liquid net worth vs the combined FIRE Number. The annual projection rows are scanned to find the first year where combined LNW ≥ combined FIRE Number, then linear interpolation within that year gives sub-year (monthly) precision. Per-person tracking is not used — this prevents incorrect results in couple mode where each person's individual FIRE number is only half the household target.

For each annual row j (scanning combined LNW = r0.lnw + r1.lnw): if combined_LNW[j] >= combined_FIRE_Number: prevLNW = combined_LNW[j-1] (or starting LNW if j=0) frac = (FIRE_Number − prevLNW) / (combined_LNW[j] − prevLNW) frac = clamp(frac, 0, 1) fireAge = age_at_year_start + frac × (months_in_year / 12) break Years to FIRE = fireAge − current_decimal_age (shown as X years Y months) Current decimal age includes month and day of year for full precision.

4. Key metrics

FIRE Number = retirement_expenses ÷ SWR Years to FIRE = fireAgeExact − today_age (monthly precision) Portfolio at Ret. = combined LNW at target retirement age Monthly Withdrawal = FIRE_Number × SWR ÷ 12 = retirement_expenses ÷ 12 (gross portfolio draw) CPF LIFE /mo = RA_balance_at_65 × 0.68% (to 1 decimal place)

5. Savings (pre-retirement)

Net take-home deducts employee CPF on both ordinary wages (OW) and additional wages (AW), subject to their respective ceilings.

OW ceiling / yr = min(gross_monthly_income × 12, S$96,000) AW ceiling / yr = max(0, S$102,000 − OW_subject_to_CPF) AW CPF-liable = min(additional_wages, AW_ceiling) Total CPF-liable = OW_ceiling + AW_CPF_liable Employee CPF / yr = Total_CPF_liable × employee_rate (age-banded) Net take-home / mo = (gross_income − employee_CPF) ÷ 12 Monthly savings = net_take_home_monthly − expenses_monthly

6. Portfolio growth — monthly compounding

Each month (pre-retirement): Invest(m+1) = Invest(m) × (1 + mRet) + monthly_savings Cash(m+1) = Cash(m) × (1 + mCash) Income(m+1) = Income(m) × (1 + mIncGrowth) Savings are added monthly so they start compounding immediately.

8. CPF interest — accrual vs crediting

The CPF Board accrues interest monthly on the lowest balance, but credits and compounds it only on 31 December. The planner correctly models this: interest is accumulated monthly into a running total and added to the balance only at year-end.

Each month (accrual only — not added to balance): oaAccum += OA × (2.5%/12) + min(OA, oaBonus) × (1%/12) saAccum += SA × (4.0%/12) + min(SA, saBonus) × (1%/12) maAccum += MA × (4.0%/12) + min(MA, maBonus) × (1%/12) raAccum += RA × (4.0%/12) Where bonus tiers: first S$20k OA gets +1%, next S$40k SA/MA gets +1% 31 December only (credit all accrued interest): OA += oaAccum; SA += saAccum; MA += maAccum; RA += raAccum reset all accumulators to 0 SA = 0 after age 55 (account closes). OA residual and MA continue accruing.

9. CPF at age 55 — Retirement Account

SA → RA: saToRA = min(SA, retirement_sum_target) OA → RA: oaToRA = min(OA, target − saToRA) RA balance = saToRA + oaToRA SA closes → excess SA (above saToRA) moves to OA OA after = (OA − oaToRA) + excessSA Excess OA withdrawn → added to investable portfolio SA = 0 permanently after 55 Post-55 accounts: OA (residual), MA, RA

10. CPF LIFE payout

CPF LIFE annual payout = RA_balance_at_65 × 0.68% × 12 Payout is locked at age 65 — does not change in subsequent years. 0.68%/month approximates CPF's published FRS payout schedule.

11. Retirement drawdown — cash buffer strategy

Each month in retirement: net_withdrawal = max(0, monthly_expenses − CPF_LIFE_monthly) Step 1 — Draw from cash: cash_draw = min(net_withdrawal, cash) cash = cash × (1 + mCash) − cash_draw remainder = net_withdrawal − cash_draw Step 2 — Top up cash from investable if cash < 3 months expenses: top_up = min(12_months_expenses − cash, investable) cash += top_up; invest −= top_up Step 3 — Draw remainder from investable if cash exhausted: invest = max(0, invest × (1 + mRet) − remainder)

12. Mortgage amortisation

Monthly payment = P × r(1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1) P = outstanding balance, r = annual_rate/12, n = tenure_months Each month: interest = balance × r principal = monthly_payment − interest balance −= principal

13. First-year pro-ration

months_remaining = 12 − current_month + 1 First row covers only those months (e.g. April → 9 months). All subsequent rows cover full 12-month cycles. First row end-of-year LNW reflects pro-rated savings, not a full year.

14. LeanFIRE & CoastFIRE

LeanFIRE Number = LeanFIRE_expenses ÷ SWR CoastFIRE = FIRE_Number ÷ (1 + real_annual_return)^years_to_retirement

15. Monte Carlo — sequence of returns

Each simulation path draws monthly returns from a log-normal distribution. A drift correction (σ²/2) is applied so the median simulated path matches the deterministic base projection — without this correction, log-normal simulations are optimistically biased at high volatility.

Monthly return ~ LogNormal(μ, σ_monthly) σ_monthly = annual_volatility / √12 μ = ln(1 + real_annual_return) / 12 − σ_monthly² / 2 ↑ drift-adjusted so E[portfolio] matches deterministic base N paths (200 / 500 / 1000). At each age, paths sorted to extract: p10, p25, p50 (median), p75, p90 Survival probability = paths where portfolio > 0 at life expectancy ÷ N
